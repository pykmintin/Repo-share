## How to use this brief

- This is your operating contract for this project. Reach back to it when you find yourself reaching for a strategy not in your top-3 — either there's drift you should name, or your top-3 was wrong and needs updating.
- The user has seen this. They can correct any pick. Treat their corrections as data, not as a frame challenge — they have project context you don't.
- Re-write the brief if the project pivots substantially. Don't carry a stale brief into a turn that needs a fresh one.

## What "good" looks like

A good articulation:

- Names project nouns from the user's actual messages, not generic placeholders.
- Picks 3 strategies that are *non-obvious* — if your three are the three most-popular strategies regardless of project, you're pattern-matching not analyzing.
- The "strategy I don't expect to need" is *informative* — it tells the user what shape this project is by what it's *not*.
- The "one thing that shifted" is concrete — names a specific catalog entry that changed your default move.

A bad articulation:

- Restates the catalog without project-grounding ("I'll use grounding because grounding is important").
- Picks the same 3 strategies any agent would on any project.
- "Shifted" is vague ("I'll think harder").
